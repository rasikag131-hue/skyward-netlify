A POCKET WINDOW TO SPACE

Let's look up.

Skyward overlays calculated positions. It doesn't detect satellites in the camera image, zoom into space, or show video from a satellite.

On your phone

  1. Open this website in a top-level HTTPS tab, preferably Safari on iPhone or Chrome on Android. An embedded preview can block camera or sensor permissions.
  2. Tap Open camera. Allow the rear camera, your location, and motion/orientation when asked.
  3. Point your phone upward. Markers move as satellites orbit and as you turn your phone. If the compass isn't supported, use manual aiming in Settings.
  4. Tap a marker or an object in the list to see its altitude, distance, and approximate pass timing.

Make the alignment better

Move away from metal and magnets. Calibrate your compass with a figure-eight movement, then align it against a known landmark using the compass correction slider. Compass errors, magnetic declination, estimated camera field of view, and old orbital elements can all shift a marker.

What can you actually see?

A visible satellite is usually a tiny point of reflected sunlight. Clear weather, a dark sky, and a sunlit satellite help. Being above the horizon is not a visibility guarantee. The “Bright” filter is the CelesTrak visual catalog, not a live brightness measurement.

Explore without a camera

The illustrative sky uses the same orbital calculations. Drag to look around, use the arrow keys, select a satellite, or tap “Locate in sky” to center it. Preview initially uses Colombo, not your actual location.

Stay aware of your surroundings. Never point optics at the Sun. This is an approximate educational tracker, not a navigation or collision-avoidance tool.
REAL DATA. OPEN SKIES.

Behind the orbit.

CelesTrak orbital elements

Public two-line elements for visual objects, stations, Starlink, and science satellites. SatNOGS is the fallback if all CelesTrak requests fail.

celestrak.org

OSIRIS catalog enrichment

Satellite mission metadata comes from OSIRIS. Its API gives position snapshots, so orbital elements are used for continuous camera tracking instead of freezing those snapshots.

OSIRIS API documentation

SGP4, calculated locally

satellite.js propagates orbits in a background worker and transforms them into azimuth and elevation at your location, approximately every 1.2 seconds. Prediction time comes from your device clock.

Loading source information…

Orbital catalogs are cached on the server for two hours. Objects with elements older than 30 days or invalid propagation are excluded. Orbit predictions still have uncertainty; no apparent-magnitude or weather model is used.

The preview sky image is illustrative and AI-generated. The camera view, when enabled, is your real local camera.
LOOK UP, NOT OVER YOUR SHOULDER

Private by design.

Your camera stays yours.

Video is displayed only in your browser. Skyward does not upload it, record it, or request microphone access. Tap “Stop camera” to release it.

Your coordinates stay on your device.

GPS is passed to the local orbital worker, never to the server or upstream providers. You can enter coordinates manually or use the labeled preview location.

You control every permission.

Camera, GPS, and phone orientation are requested only after you tap a corresponding control. The page stops orbital work when hidden and releases the camera when closed. Revoke permissions anytime in your browser's site settings.

No application analytics, cookies, or account are used. Like any hosted website, the hosting provider may receive standard request logs, including your IP address. Only public orbital data is fetched by the backend.
